# Game Concept: Star Haul

*Created: 2026-11-26*
*Status: Draft*

*"Star Haul" is a working title.*

---

## Elevator Pitch

> It's a first-person co-op extraction game where you fly out from your
> homestead into a bright toy-box solar system, grab everything you can carry,
> and try to get home alive, because whatever you're holding when you die stays
> there.

---

## Core Fantasy

You are the space scavenger who always pushes one room further than is
sensible, and gets rich doing it.

The emotional promise is risk paying off: the hard boss finally going down, the
airlock closing behind you with a full pack. Wealth here is earned under
pressure and then lived in. You walk through it at home.

**In the designer's words:** "a first person colorful space world with farming,
crafting and building houses, taking a spaceship to various moons planets and
asteroids"; "possible to stay on your home planet or home space station the
whole game just crafting and farming but encouraged to venture to the planets
higher risk higher reward"; "a game that does it all"; "feeling like im getting
rich".

---

## Unique Hook

It's like a hardcore extraction game, **and also** every destination is a
different trade: asteroids are mining runs, moons are stealth heists, planets
are melee extraction runs with bosses. All three pay into one stash, one
crafting economy and one home you build yourself.

The alternative considered was a single run type with more depth. It was
rejected because the three trades are the designer's core idea; the scope cost
is handled by building one trade at a time (Pillar 4).

## Player Experience Analysis (MDA Framework)

### Target Aesthetics (What the player FEELS)

| Aesthetic | Priority | How We Deliver It |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **Challenge** (obstacle course, mastery) | 1 | Extract-or-lose stakes, bosses, guard routes and hazards that are learned |
| **Fantasy** (make-believe, role-playing) | 2 | The scavenger getting rich; a homestead in space |
| **Discovery** (exploration, secrets) | 3 | New destinations unlocked by the ship; routes, seams and secrets on each |
| **Expression** (self-expression, creativity) | 4 | Building and arranging the home; choosing a trade and a loadout |
| **Fellowship** (social connection) | 5 | Co-op crews sharing risk and haul; showing friends your home |
| **Sensation** (sensory pleasure) | 6 | Loot that glows against a pastel world; weighty melee hits |
| **Submission** (relaxation, comfort zone) | 7 | Farming and crafting at home between runs, with no timers |
| **Narrative** (drama, story arc) | N/A | No story campaign; stories are the runs players tell each other |

### Key Dynamics (Emergent player behaviors)

- Players debate "one more room or leave now" on every run, alone and aloud with their crew.
- Players scout before committing: watching patrols, reading a boss, checking a seam.
- Players plan runs around a home goal ("two more of these and I can build the forge").
- Players specialise in a trade, then cross over because another trade holds a material they need.
- Stay-home players supply crews with food, gear and tools, and process what crews bring back.

### Core Mechanics (Systems we build)

1. **Extraction runs:** a limited pack, exits, and loss of everything carried on death; the home stash is always safe.
2. **Three trades:** asteroid mining, moon stealth heists, planet melee combat with bosses.
3. **Home:** farming, crafting workshops, a player-built house and a persistent stash.
4. **Progression:** gear tiers crafted from run materials; ship upgrades that unlock further, more dangerous destinations.
5. **Co-op:** host-authoritative crews against AI.

## Player Motivation Profile

### Primary Psychological Needs Served

| Need | How This Game Satisfies It | Strength |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **Autonomy** (freedom, meaningful choice) | The player chooses the trade, the stake, when to leave, and whether to run at all | Core |
| **Competence** (mastery, skill growth) | Guard routes, boss patterns and ore seams are learned; a veteran clears what a beginner can't | Core |
| **Relatedness** (connection, belonging) | Co-op crews share risk and haul; the home is something to show | Supporting |

### Player Type Appeal (Bartle Taxonomy)

- [x] **Achievers** (goal completion, collection, progression) — How: the stash, gear tiers, the boss ladder, a visibly richer home. Primary.
- [x] **Explorers** (discovery, understanding systems, finding secrets) — How: new destinations, learning each one's routes and secrets. Secondary.
- [x] **Socializers** (relationships, cooperation, community) — How: co-op crews; supplying friends from home. Secondary.
- [ ] **Killers/Competitors** (domination, PvP, leaderboards) — Not served. There is no player-versus-player play.

Creators (Quantic Foundry's Creativity motive) are a secondary audience through
home building.

**Not for:** players who want player-versus-player competition, a written story
to follow, a purely cozy game with nothing to lose, or slow turn-based strategy.

### Flow State Design

- **Onboarding curve**: the first run is a short asteroid mining trip with one tool and one hazard. It teaches the whole game in miniature: fill the pack, watch the danger rise, get out.
- **Difficulty scaling**: the player sets it. Further destinations and deeper areas are more dangerous and richer; nothing forces the step up.
- **Feedback clarity**: wealth is visible. The pack brightens with loot, the stash fills, the home grows.
- **Recovery from failure**: death costs what was carried and nothing else. The stash and home are untouched, so a new run can start at once with spare gear. Failure stings (Pillar 3) but never ends a save.

## Core Loop

### Moment-to-Moment (30 seconds)

You move carefully through a hostile place, listening and watching. You open a
container and weigh what to take against limited pack space, then decide whether
the next room is worth it. A fight is a few committed swings, blocks and dodges
where stamina and timing decide it.

The feel is **tense and deliberate**. It satisfies on its own because every
action has a visible cost and a readable result: noise draws attention, a missed
swing leaves you open, a full pack slows you down.

### Short-Term (5-15 minutes)

Scout, commit, loot, reassess. Each cycle ends on the same choice: push deeper
for better loot, or head for the exit with what you have. The pack fills while
health and supplies drain, so pressure rises with the value carried. "One more
room" lives here.

### Session-Level (30-120 minutes)

Prepare at home (craft gear, pack food from the farm, pick a destination and a
trade) → fly out → the run → extract or die → unpack, sell, craft, build, plant
→ pick the next run.

| Trade | Destination | Run length | Character |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Mining | Asteroids | 10 to 15 minutes | Quick material runs you can chain; the risk is staying for one more seam |
| Heists | Moons | 20 to 30 minutes | Slow, careful stealth |
| Extraction | Planets | 30 to 45+ minutes | Big maps, melee fights and a boss; highest stakes, biggest haul |

The natural stopping point is at home after unpacking. The reason to come back
is a crop that will be ready, a recipe one part short, or a boss you now have
the gear to try.

### Long-Term Progression

Wealth grows along four tracks:

1. **Stash and money**
2. **Gear tier**, crafted from run materials
3. **Home**: workshops, farm plots and the house itself, which unlock recipes and storage
4. **Ship**, which unlocks further and more dangerous destinations

The long-term goal is the richest home and the hardest bosses beaten. Staying
home is a slower, safe income inside the same economy: farming and crafting make
the food, gear and tools that runs use, and process what runs bring back.

### Retention Hooks

- **Curiosity**: destinations the ship can't reach yet; deeper areas and bosses not yet seen
- **Investment**: a stash and a home built from survived runs
- **Social**: a crew to run with; friends to supply and show the home to
- **Mastery**: routes, patterns and seams to learn; harder destinations to clear

## Game Pillars

### Pillar 1: The haul is the heartbeat
Every system exists to make loot more desirable or getting it home more tense.
Home is part of this: farming and crafting feed the haul.

*Design test*: Debating a feature that doesn't touch the haul or one that does,
this pillar picks the one that does.

### Pillar 2: Risk is a choice
The player always decides how much to stake. Danger is always readable and can
be scouted before it is lethal. Heists and bosses may keep secrets; a careful
player could always have seen it coming.

*Design test*: Debating a surprise that kills an unwarned player or a danger
they could have seen coming, this pillar picks the visible danger.

### Pillar 3: Bright world, real stakes
It looks friendly and colourful; it plays seriously.

*Design test*: Debating softening a loss to match the art or keeping the sting,
this pillar picks the sting.

### Pillar 4: Three trades, one fortune
Stealth, melee and mining play differently but pay into the same stash, crafting
and home. A trade ships only when it plays well alone. Build order: mining,
then heists, then planets.

*Design test*: Debating a material only one trade uses or one that links two,
this pillar picks the link.

### Pillar 5: Home is the scoreboard
Wealth is something you see and walk through, not a number.

*Design test*: Debating a stat upgrade in a menu or a workshop you build and
stand in, this pillar picks the workshop.

### Anti-Pillars (What This Game Is NOT)

- **NOT player-versus-player raids**: they would turn chosen risk into imposed risk (Pillar 2) and multiply the build.
- **NOT mandatory home upkeep** (crops dying, bills due): it would turn the scoreboard into a job (Pillar 5).
- **NOT a story campaign with fixed missions**: it would pull the game's centre away from the haul (Pillar 1).
- **NOT gear sold or granted outside play**: wealth must mean something (Pillars 1 and 3).
- **NOT a mandatory run cadence or timers between runs**: the player runs when they choose, which protects the stay-home path (Pillar 2).

## Visual Identity Anchor

**Direction: Cargo Is the Colour**

> **Updated 2026-11-26.** The look is now stylised realism, chosen by the
> designer after playing the prototype. `design/art/art-bible.md` Section 1 is
> the current word on the look; the rule and the three principles below are
> unchanged.

**Visual rule:** *The most saturated thing on screen is always something you
could carry home.*

Supporting principles:

1. **Loot owns saturation and gloss.** The world is smooth, matte and naturally coloured: textured rock in stone colours with a soft pastel cast, under real light and shadow; loot is the only faceted, glossy, fully saturated geometry, and rarity reads as saturation. *Design test*: debating a vivid environment prop or a muted one, pick the muted one, so loot keeps the eye.
2. **One hue means danger.** Hot magenta is reserved for threat and is used for nothing else. *Design test*: debating magenta for a decorative flower or a rare gem, the answer is no; pick another hue.
3. **Home brightens with wealth.** As the stash fills and the home is built, the home becomes the most colourful place in the game. *Design test*: debating showing wealth as a counter or as displayed, coloured objects, pick the objects.

**Colour philosophy:** brightness comes from value and lighting, not chroma. The
world is sunlit, matte and calm; desire is where the colour is. Tension rises as
the pack gets brighter.

**Trade-off accepted:** limiting environment saturation pulls slightly against
"bright" in Pillar 3. The alternatives considered were *Painted Tin* (a painted
toy world where worn bare metal marks danger; strongest identity, more work per
asset) and *Noon Shadows* (high-key, hard shadows, one hue per trade; depends on
lighting quality). Noon Shadows' per-trade hues (mining amber, heist teal, melee
coral) can be layered on later as accent lighting.

## Risks and Open Questions

### Design Risks
- Three trades are about three games' worth of design. Mitigation: Pillar 4's rule and build order.
- Home could become a menu between runs. Mitigation: Pillar 5; home feeds the haul.
- A harsh loss could feel wrong in a friendly-looking world. Pillar 3 chooses the sting; needs playtesting.
- The stay-home path may be too slow to feel worthwhile, or so good that nobody runs.

### Technical Risks
- **Co-op cannot be bolted on later.** Decision taken: build for it from day one. All inventory, stash, crafting and world changes go through host-side checks even in solo play, with game state kept apart from presentation. A short ADR on the network model comes before any MVP code.
- **Networked melee** needs lag compensation and host-confirmed hits. Prototype with simulated latency before Tier 3.
- **Persistence in co-op**: whose home and stash it is, and stopping loot duplication when guests carry items between hosts. Decide before Tier 2a.
- **Save migration**: patches can break player-built homes. Version save data from the MVP onward.
- **Large worlds**: float precision far from the origin (ship flight, planets).
- **AI under netcode**: guard perception runs on the host only.
- **Testing multiplayer alone**: needs multiple local instances and a latency simulator early.

### Market Risks
- Competition in the extraction genre: not researched.
- Size of the audience for co-op-only extraction without player-versus-player: not researched.
- This is a hobby project; no commercial target is set.

### Scope Risks
- Full vision is a multi-year goal for one developer, not a commitment. Each tier must stand as a finished game.
- This is the designer's first first-person 3D game after two finished mobile games; the MVP will take longer than it looks, mostly on controller feel and hazard tuning.
- Bosses, animation and big maps cost a lot even in low-poly.
- Most likely cut point: partway through Tier 1. Splitting it into 1a and 1b makes that a graceful stop.

### Open Questions
- Crew size for co-op. Answer during `/design-system` for co-op, before Tier 2a.
- What the worsening hazard on an asteroid is. Answer with the MVP prototype.
- Does death also cost equipped gear, or only the pack? Current rule: everything carried. Test in the MVP.
- Home planet or home space station, or the player's choice of either?
- How a stay-home player earns: trading with crews, a market, or both?

## MVP Definition

**Core hypothesis**: "One more seam or leave now" is tense on a 10 to 15 minute
asteroid mining run, and unpacking the haul at home is satisfying enough to make
the player go straight back out.

**Required for MVP**:
1. One asteroid, one mining tool, and a pack with limited space
2. A hazard that worsens the longer you stay
3. An exit; die before reaching it and the pack is lost
4. A home with a persistent stash and one crafting bench
5. Solo play running as a host with one player, with versioned save data

**Explicitly NOT in MVP** (defer to later):
- Co-op play itself (the architecture allows for it; the feature is Tier 2a)
- Farming and house building
- Heists, melee combat, bosses
- The ship as a flyable vehicle and the destination map

### Scope Tiers (if budget/time shrinks)

Each tier is a finished game on its own and adds one new risk. If time runs out,
the last completed tier ships. Timelines are estimates, solo, and were not
assessed by the producer.

| Tier | Content | Features | Timeline (estimate) |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **MVP** | One asteroid | Mining run, pack, hazard, exit, stash, one bench. 20 systems; see `design/gdd/systems-index.md` | 9 to 12 months (producer's estimate, low confidence; originally 2 to 4) |
| **1a. The mining game** | Several asteroids | Tool and gear tiers, crafting, stash. The first finish line | Not estimated |
| **1b. Home and ship** | Home plot, destination map | Basic farming, buildable home, ship | Not estimated |
| **2a. Crew** | Existing mining content | Online co-op | Not estimated |
| **2b. Heists** | Moons | Guard AI, stealth | Not estimated |
| **3. Planets** | Large planet maps | Melee combat, bosses | Not estimated |
| **4. Full Vision** | Many destinations per trade | Deep progression across gear, home, ship and farm; full house building | 4 to 6+ years in total |
